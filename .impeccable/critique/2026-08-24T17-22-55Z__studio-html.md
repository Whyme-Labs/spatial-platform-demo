## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The Studio feels authored for spatial production rather than interchangeable with another dashboard. Field-dark surfaces, warm evidence text, mono provenance labels, line-led containment, and scarce Survey Lime express the Spatial Fieldbook with real product specificity. The strongest product-specific design behavior is not decorative: stage, blocker, evidence, retry, review, and revocation are visible concepts in the interface.

**Deterministic scan:** The detector returned exit 0 and `[]`, but this is not a clean bill of health. Required parser modules (`htmlparser2`, `css-select`, `css-tree`, and `domutils`) were unavailable, so it fell back to regex matching and did not evaluate custom properties, selector matching, computed contrast, or full DOM semantics. There were zero reported findings and therefore zero detector false positives.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay exists. Mutable browser injection succeeded, but the deployed Content Security Policy blocked `http://localhost:8400/detect.js` with `blockedReason: "csp"`. The live browser confirmed the production title and signed-out sign-in dialog, but authenticated project, processing, review, and publishing workspaces were unavailable. The design review therefore combined current source with `docs/previews/operations-studio.png`; it did not invent authenticated behavior.

## Overall Impression

This is a credible, unusually specific operational interface with strong evidence semantics. Its biggest opportunity is not a visual rebrand; it is editorial control over attention. The Studio knows the current stage and blocker, yet the home surface still makes portfolio metrics, the project list, and the processing queue compete before an operator reaches the single consequential task.

## Emotional Journey

The opening is confident and serious: “Capture to preview,” a dark instrument surface, and exact immutable-state language establish trust. The middle risks becoming an anxiety valley because multiple operational signals ask, “Where should I look?” at once. The strongest potential ending is already present in the product model—reviewed evidence, an immutable release, a URL, and revocation—but the shell does not yet make that verified handoff the unmistakable peak/end moment.

## Cognitive Load

Three of the eight checklist dimensions fail or remain materially weak: **single focus**, **one thing at a time**, and **minimal choices**. Grouping, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure are otherwise strong; Advanced ingest is correctly collapsed and file purpose is inferred when possible.

Decision points above the four-option working-memory boundary:

- Project workspace navigation exposes six baseline sections—Overview, Process, Structure, Publish, Measurement evidence, and Expert—with Compare becoming a seventh when eligible.
- The project list presents five status filters before search, sort, and the Filters and saved views disclosure.
- The 14 asset-purpose choices are behind Advanced ingest options and usually inferred from the file, so they are not counted as a default-flow failure; preserve that progressive disclosure.

## What's Working

- **The design language is product-specific.** The field-dark palette, evidence typography, line-led panels, and signal colors make this feel like spatial-production infrastructure rather than generic SaaS.
- **Consequential state is modeled explicitly.** The project context bar names Current stage, Blocker, and one current action; notices and form errors use live regions; processing work exposes retry and durable-job language.
- **The interface supports real operators.** Saved views, bulk actions, resumable uploads, sorting, filtering, pagination, and hash-addressable project sections provide efficiency without moving critical validation into an opaque automation layer.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Home attention is divided across three primary work signals

**Why it matters:** Four summary cards appear before the project board and processing queue, while the queue also contains an architecture statement. Operators have to decide whether metrics, projects, or jobs represent the real next action.

**Evidence:** [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:106) defines four equal summary cards; [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:113) then places the project board beside the processing queue; [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:189) gives the queue its own task surface and architecture card.

**Fix:** Lead with one “Needs attention” or “Next production action” surface derived from blocker and current-action state. Keep the project list as the main working surface; demote storage/release counts and compute architecture to secondary disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill studio.html`

### [P1] Project navigation exposes implementation categories instead of a short journey

**Why it matters:** Six baseline sections, with a seventh conditional Compare section, force operators to learn where capabilities live. “Measurement evidence” and “Expert” are valid capabilities but weak top-level wayfinding concepts.

**Evidence:** [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:91) defines Overview, Process, Structure, conditional Compare, Publish, Measurement evidence, and Expert, then mirrors them in the compact picker.

**Fix:** Shape three durable journey groups—Work, Evidence, and Publish—while preserving current deep links underneath. Put Compare and Measurement evidence inside Evidence, and make Expert an explicit advanced escape hatch rather than a peer lifecycle stage.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape project workspace navigation`

### [P2] The project list front-loads too many refinement controls

**Why it matters:** Five status chips plus search, sort, and advanced filters create a second attention contest before the operator has selected work. Saved views are valuable, but their power should not raise the default decision count.

**Evidence:** [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:117) presents five status filters; [studio.html](/Users/sohweimeng/Documents/projects/spatial-platform-demo/studio.html:125) follows with search, sort, capture-source filtering, delivery classification, and saved-view controls.

**Fix:** Keep Current and search visible. Move status, sort, capture source, delivery class, and saved views into one Refine control that summarizes active constraints and offers one-step reset.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill project list controls`
